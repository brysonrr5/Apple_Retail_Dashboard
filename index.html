Key insights for managers

Based on all data, Jan 2020 – Oct 2024. Use the filters on the Dashboard page to explore each point.

  1. Revenue is flat, not growing

    Jan–Oct revenue stayed between $1.05B and $1.06B every year from 2020 to 2024 (2024: +0.9% vs. 2023), with no holiday spike in Q4. Implication: growth will have to come from new stores or a better product mix. Staffing and inventory can be planned on a steady monthly baseline.

  2. Monthly differences come from the number of days

    February has the lowest average monthly total ($98.2M vs. $108.6M in March), but revenue per day is $3.43M–$3.50M in every month, so the dip is simply the shorter month (pick a single year to see the pattern in the monthly trend chart). Implication: no month is a true peak or slump. Compare months on a per-day basis before acting on a "slow month".

  3. Every location performs alike, so country totals reflect store count

    Revenue per store per month is $1.38M–$1.43M in all 19 countries. The US earns 20% of revenue because it has 20% of the stores (15 of 75). Implication: no market is under- or over-performing per store. Expansion decisions should rest on where to add stores, not on which existing stores to fix.

  4. The real gap is between individual products

    The top product (Apple Music, $124.8M) earned 8× the bottom product (MacBook (Retina), $14.8M). Category totals mostly follow how many products each category has. Implication: review the bottom 10 products for promotion or discontinuation; that's where the mix can change.

  5. About half of 2024 warranty claims are still open

    Claims are steady at 2.7%–3.0% of sales in every category, but 51% are still Pending or In Progress, and 25% were Rejected. Implication: the issue is service capacity, not product quality. Clearing the repair backlog should be a priority.

Data notes and limitations

  • Source: Apple Retail Sales dataset (Kaggle): 1,040,200 sales, 89 products, 75 stores, 30,000 warranty claims.
  • Excluded: 6,982 sales from 1–12 Nov 2024 (the data stops mid-month) and the 203 warranty claims tied to them. December 2024 is not in the data.
  • Revenue uses each product's list price; discounts and actual selling prices are not in the data.
  • Duplicate names: six store names (e.g., Apple Chadstone) and "HomePod mini" appear under two different IDs. They are kept separate and labeled with their store ID or category.
  • Launch dates look unreliable: 48% of sales are dated before the product's listed launch date. These sales are kept but should be treated with caution.
  • Warranty: all claims are dated in 2024, and 2,488 are dated before their sale date. Claim rate means the share of sales with a claim filed in 2024.
  • The sales are spread very evenly across stores and months, which suggests the dataset is synthetic. Patterns may not reflect real Apple retail behavior.
